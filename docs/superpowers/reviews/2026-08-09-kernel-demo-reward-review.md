# Reward-Design Review — Kernel Demo (`2026-08-09-kernel-demo-design.md`, rev 2)

**Reviewer:** reward-function-reviewer (yzmir-deep-rl) · **Date:** 2026-08-09
**Subject:** reward design only. Scope pins treated as owner-approved and not
relitigated. Nothing below asks for host families, lifecycle knobs, extra
seeds, or shaped reward terms.

---

## Reward Function Review

### Alignment ⚠️
The WHICH objective (`J = Σ_a π(a|s,NOW)·R_a`) is genuinely aligned — it is an
exact full-information contextual-bandit objective over measured
counterfactuals, and it is the right shape. The WHEN objective is not: its
reward `R_chosen − R_noop` is a function of the *other head's current
competence*, and the action it scores is the action that decides whether the
WHICH head gets training data at all. The two heads are coupled through both
the reward and the data supply, in the same direction.

### Scale ❌
Reward units are raw val-accuracy differences: signal magnitude ~10⁻²,
plausibly ~5×10⁻³. Two entropy coefficients are called for ("a small entropy
term", "small entropy bonus") with no value and no calibration rule, and the
two heads' gradients differ in scale by roughly 5× in opposite directions. At
conventional defaults the entropy term is the same order as the entire reward
signal.

### Sparsity ⚠️/❌
WHICH is dense and fully labelled — excellent, and the design's whole point.
WHEN is one terminal scalar for a whole WAIT-prefix, trained online only, on
episodes the policy itself decides to generate. The asymmetry is acknowledged
in the spec but the mitigation (a warm-up plus an assertion) does not close it.

### Hacking Potential ⚠️
No classical reward-gaming channel — end-state-only reward on a held-out
metric, with no shaped terms, closes the Esper failure class cleanly. The
exposure is different in kind: **`R_a` can be corrupted by arm mismatch**, so
the policy learns artefacts of the harness rather than gaming a term. §Fan
step 2 fixes data RNG but not model RNG or gradient-clipping coupling.

### Component Balance ❌
There is no stated weighting between the offline WHICH loss, the online WHEN
loss, and the two entropy terms, all flowing into one shared trunk.

### F1 — Two-head coupling + data-starvation loop · **Critical**

**Spec text at fault** — §Learning:
> **WHEN — online.** The NOW/WAIT decisions train by plain REINFORCE on fresh
> episodes only, reward `R_chosen − R_noop` […]

and §The counterfactual fan:
> **Never-germinate episodes** produce no fan and train nothing […]

Three defects compound into one loop:

1. **The WHEN reward is non-stationary in WHICH's competence.** `R_chosen` is
   the *sampled* seed's outcome. Early in training π_which ≈ uniform, so
   E[R_chosen − R_noop] ≈ mean_a(R_a) − R_noop. The pathology table designs
   exactly one winner per pathology, so under a uniform WHICH the other three
   arms drag the mean down; on mild-handicap episodes (where no-op is
   designed to win) it is negative by construction. WHEN is therefore trained
   on evidence that acting is worthless, generated by WHICH's immaturity.
2. **WHEN's action gates WHICH's data supply.** Suppressing NOW produces no
   fan, so WHICH stops improving, so E[R_chosen − R_noop] stays low.
3. **The absorbing state is reward-free and variance-free.** Never-germinate
   returns exactly 0 with zero variance. Every alternative has negative
   expected value early and high variance. REINFORCE converges to it.

The spec's two guards do not close this. The offline warm-up trains WHICH on
whatever store exists, but the only stated pre-policy collection is "~30
random-policy episodes" for pre-flight — the spec never commits that this
store is large enough for WHICH to reach positive expected lift before online
WHEN training begins. And the guard —

> assert during online training that the NOW head's outputs actually move
> (logged, checked in `--train`)

— is satisfied by monotone collapse toward zero. It detects a *frozen* head,
not a *collapsing* one. **There is no exploration floor.** The NOW entropy
bonus is the only pressure against collapse, and per F2 it is the weaker of
the two entropy applications at any shared coefficient.

**Fix (primary, and this is the review's main recommendation): train the NOW
decision offline on full information too.** At a *measured* decision point the
NOW decision is a two-arm full-information bandit, exactly parallel to WHICH:

```
J_now(s_t) = p(s_t) · ( Σ_a π_which(a|s_t) · R_a )  +  (1 − p(s_t)) · R_noop
```

Every term is already in the fan record. Concretely:

- During `--collect`, sample the germination epoch from a fixed exploration
  schedule (uniform over the decision window, or ε-mixed with the policy),
  **not** from the NOW head. Data supply is then decoupled from the policy.
- Train both heads offline on the accumulated store. Recompute
  `A(s_t) = Σ_a π_which(a|s_t)·R_a − R_noop` as π_which improves, so the WHEN
  signal tracks WHICH's competence instead of lagging it.

This kills all three defects at once: the starvation loop (supply is
scheduled), the non-stationarity (A is recomputed, not stale), and the
WAIT-prefix credit smearing in F1b below. It is not a scope widening — the
offline/online split lives in §Learning, not in §Scope pins, and it *increases*
fidelity to the design's own stated principle ("dense signal in a tiny action
space").

**F1b — WAIT-prefix credit smearing (subsumed by the fix above, but stated
separately in case the online path is retained).** Under plain REINFORCE with
one terminal return G, the trajectory `WAIT^k, NOW` receives
`G·(Σ_{i<k} ∇log(1−p_i) + ∇log p_k)`. No counterfactual was ever measured for
those WAIT epochs; they are reinforced solely because the germination that
followed them happened to score well. This is self-confirming: whatever
germination epoch the initial policy happens to sample gets reinforced, and
competing epochs are never evaluated. Expect timing lock-in, not timing
learning. If the online path is retained, at minimum do not credit WAIT
actions with the terminal return — restrict the gradient to the NOW step.

### F2 — Entropy coefficients unspecified and uncalibrated; one β cannot serve both heads · **Critical**

**Spec text at fault** — §Learning:
> plus a small entropy term to delay premature saturation
> […] small entropy bonus.

No coefficient is given and no calibration rule is stated, while the reward is
in raw val-accuracy units where the entire between-arm signal is ~10⁻².

**WHICH head.** With a softmax over 4 seeds, the reward gradient on logit *i*
is `π_i·(R_i − R̄)`, i.e. **damped by π**. At a moderately sharpened
π ≈ (0.7, 0.1, 0.1, 0.1) and a fan spread of 0.01, that is ~2×10⁻³, while the
entropy gradient at the same point is ~4×10⁻¹. The two cross somewhere in the
10⁻³ range — order-of-magnitude, not a threshold. The consequence: **any
conventional default (β = 10⁻²) is the same order as or larger than the whole
reward signal**, and pins the WHICH head near-uniform. At fan densities below
~10⁻² it never leaves uniform at all.

**WHEN head.** The REINFORCE gradient is `G·∇log p` — **undamped**, magnitude
~10⁻². The Bernoulli entropy gradient at a collapsing p ≈ 0.02 is ~8×10⁻².
Here the same β is roughly an order of magnitude *too weak* to arrest F1's
collapse.

So a single "small entropy bonus" is simultaneously too strong for WHICH and
too weak for WHEN. This is not a tuning inconvenience; it is the mechanism by
which F1's loop is left unguarded.

**Fix.** Two separately-named coefficients, both expressed in units the design
already measures. §Pre-flight already computes **fan density**
`mean(R_best − R_worst)` — use it:

```
β_which = c_which · fan_density        # c_which ≈ 0.05–0.1, annealed to 0
β_when  = c_when  · fan_density        # calibrated separately
```

Equivalently, standardise `R_a` within-fan to unit spread before the objective
and use absolute β values — same effect, easier to reason about. Log the ratio
`‖∇_entropy‖ / ‖∇_reward‖` at each head every update and assert it stays inside
a declared band; a silent 100× imbalance is exactly the class of defect this
project reset to make unrepresentable.

**Note on WHICH entropy specifically:** it is not merely mis-scaled, it may be
unnecessary. Entropy exists to preserve exploration, and **a full-information
objective has nothing to explore** — every unchosen arm is already labelled.
See F7 for the damage it does downstream.

### F3 — Arm-matching integrity: model RNG and gradient clipping are unguarded · **Critical**

**Spec text at fault** — §The counterfactual fan, step 2:
> the remaining epochs' batch index order *and all augmentation decisions*
> (crop offsets, flip masks) are drawn once, independent of model RNG, and
> shared by every arm.

The reasoning here is correct and the data-RNG fix is exactly right. But it
covers the data stream only. Two other channels desynchronise the arms and
directly corrupt `R_a` — and a corrupted `R_a` invalidates every downstream
number, both heads' objectives, and the headline claim.

**(a) Model RNG.** If the host contains dropout (or any stochastic layer), the
arms consume the global generator differently the moment a seed module is
present — and `attn` in particular may itself draw. The host's dropout masks
then differ across arms, so the arms are no longer matched and the measured
difference contains ordinary-training variance, which is precisely the variance
the counterfactual apparatus exists to cancel.
**Fix:** a dedicated generator per arm, seeded identically, for all model
stochasticity; or precompute dropout masks into the common future alongside the
augmentation decisions.

**(b) Global gradient-norm clipping.** If host and seed parameters are clipped
under one global norm, the seed's gradients change the clip scale and therefore
perturb the *host's* update — silently breaking matching in exactly the arms
that carry the largest seeds (`conv_heavy`).
**Fix:** clip host and seed parameter groups separately, or exclude seed params
from the host clip.

**(c) The assertion that catches all of this at once.** During TRAINING the
forward is `h + (Δ − Δ.detach())`, which is bit-exact equal to `h`, and the
host's backward through the slot is the identity. Therefore:

> **Assert: at the end of the TRAINING stage, host weights are bitwise
> identical across all five arms.**

Any divergence is a harness bug — RNG desync, optimizer contamination, clipping
coupling, or an STE error — caught in one cheap check, before any reward is
recorded. Run it in pre-flight and as a periodic sampled check in `--collect`.
This is the single highest-value line to add to the spec.

### F4 — Evaluation is blind to restraint quality · **High**

**Spec text at fault** — §The counterfactual fan:
> These episodes still land in the store for evaluation statistics (restraint
> rate).

and §Measurement 1:
> trained policy's mean lift `R_chosen − R_noop` on fresh eval episodes

Never-germinate episodes produce no fan, so they contribute exactly 0 to mean
lift **and carry no counterfactual**. A 0 is therefore indistinguishable
between "correctly declined — every arm was worse than no-op" and "left +0.05
on the table." Restraint rate without a correctness denominator is a vanity
metric: it only ever goes up, and it goes up fastest for the collapsed policy
of F1. The spec's own success criterion invokes restraint
("she must learn restraint, not enthusiasm"), so the demo currently cannot
evidence its own claim.

**Fix.** On `--eval` episodes only (cheap — eval volume is small), force fan
collection regardless of the NOW decision, at the policy's argmax-NOW epoch
(or at a fixed schedule of epochs). Then report, per episode:

```
regret = max(R_noop, max_a R_a) − R_realised
```

with **no-op eligible as the optimum** and `R_realised = R_noop` for a
never-germinate episode. Mean regret against fan-optimal is the honest headline
— it scores correct restraint at 0 and missed opportunity at its true cost,
which mean lift cannot do. Keep mean lift as a secondary number.

### F5 — The common future is drawn per-fan, not per-episode · **High**

**Spec text at fault** — §The counterfactual fan, step 2:
> **Precompute the common future:** the remaining epochs' batch index order
> *and all augmentation decisions* […] are drawn once

"Once" is scoped to the germination event. Draw it **once per episode, over all
epochs 0…horizon**, instead. Three consequences, all free:

- **The no-op arm becomes literally the base run.** No 5th arm needs running
  per fan — run the base episode once and reuse `R_base`. ~20% fan-compute
  saving at identical statistical content.
- **Fans at different *t* within one episode become paired.** They share one
  baseline and one data future, so `A(s_t)` is directly comparable across *t*
  inside an episode. **This partially dissolves the design's central
  acknowledged limitation** — "now vs never, not now vs later" — at zero extra
  cost. Combined with F1's scheduled germination epochs, the store contains
  measured now-vs-later evidence.
- **As currently written, cross-*t* comparison inside an episode is invalid.**
  A fan at t=6 and a fan at t=10 draw different futures over their overlapping
  epochs, so their no-op arms differ for reasons unrelated to timing. Any
  analysis that compares them is comparing noise. This is a defect in its own
  right today, independent of the upgrades above.

### F6 — Horizon adequacy: reward magnitude may vary systematically with *t*, and nothing measures it · **High**

**Spec text at fault** — §The counterfactual fan, Horizon:
> default ~40 epochs (decision window ~5–15, TRAINING ~3, BLENDING ~3, leaving
> ≥15 fossilized epochs of runway)

With a fixed episode horizon, germinating at t=5 gives 29 fossilized epochs and
t=15 gives 19 — a 1.5× spread in post-blend runway. That could bias lift
downward in *t* (less runway to exploit new capacity). It could equally bias
upward: the spec says the host "plateaus well inside the horizon," so `R_noop`
saturates and later germination faces a flatter baseline. **The direction is
genuinely indeterminate from the spec** — it depends on whether 19 fossilized
epochs suffices for the seed arms to converge, which is not knowable here.

The finding is therefore not a direction but a **missing measurement**: the
WHEN head is trained on a reward whose magnitude may be a mechanical function
of *t*, and no pre-flight check would detect it. Under F1b's credit smearing
that mechanical gradient would be learned as if it were timing diagnosis.

**Fix — add a fourth pre-flight gate, "horizon adequacy," with two checks:**

4a. **Lift-vs-*t* flatness.** Run random-germination-epoch episodes with *t*
    uniform over the decision window; plot mean `max_a R_a − R_noop` against
    *t*. Gate: no significant monotone trend relative to fan density. If a
    trend exists, either adopt `horizon = germination_epoch + fixed_runway`
    (equalising runway across *t*, valid because every comparison is
    within-fan) or lengthen the horizon — and **do not claim timing diagnosis**
    until the gate passes.
4b. **Plateau gate.** Require the no-op arm's val-accuracy slope over the
    final averaging window to be ≈ 0. If arms are still improving at the
    horizon, `R_a` measures convergence *speed*, not end-state quality, and
    the constitution's end-state-only rule is satisfied in letter only.

### F7 — The WHICH entropy bonus directly depresses the WHEN reward · **High**

A clean causal chain entirely within the current design:

1. §Learning applies an entropy term to WHICH "to delay premature saturation."
2. Per F2, in raw accuracy units that term plausibly holds π_which near-uniform.
3. §Learning defines WHEN's reward as `R_chosen − R_noop`, where `R_chosen` is
   the **sampled** arm.
4. A near-uniform π_which means the sampled arm is usually not the best arm, so
   E[R_chosen] ≪ max_a R_a.
5. WHEN therefore observes a systematically depressed reward for acting — which
   is precisely F1's collapse driver.

The entropy bonus intended to protect WHICH's exploration is, through the
sampled-arm reward, a suppressant on WHEN.

**Fix.** Two independent moves, either of which breaks the chain, both worth
taking:
- Use the **expected** value under π_which, not the sampled arm, in WHEN's
  reward: `Σ_a π_which(a|s)·R_a − R_noop`. Every term is in the fan record;
  this also removes the sampling variance entirely. (F1's fix does this
  already.)
- Anneal β_which to zero. Full information needs no exploration bonus; if the
  term is wanted for early conditioning, it should be a decaying schedule, not
  a standing term.

### F8 — Parameter-count confound may collapse the argmax marginal; reported chance rates are then wrong · **Medium/High**

**Spec text at fault** — §Seeds table (`conv_heavy` ~60k on a ~150k host,
`norm` ~0.1k) and §Measurement 2:
> five-way agreement with the fan argmax including restraint (chance ≈ 20%),
> and conditional-on-acting seed agreement (chance ≈ 25%).

`R_a` measures end-state accuracy of host+seed. On a *deliberately undersized*
host, `conv_heavy` adds ~40% parameters; capacity alone may win most pathologies
regardless of fit. Adding a parameter-cost term would be reward shaping and is
correctly forbidden — so the burden falls on the pathology sampler, and **no
pre-flight check tests it.** Check 1 tests only that no-op wins its share;
check 3 tests contrast magnitude, not which arm wins.

If the argmax marginal is skewed (say 60% `conv_heavy`), then:
- the WHICH task is largely solvable by a constant policy;
- the money chart's diagonal can look strong for the wrong reason;
- **the quoted chance rates are wrong.** The correct null is the
  **majority-class rate**, not 25%/20%. A 55%-accurate policy against a 60%
  majority class is *below* chance while reading as "well above 25%."

**Fix.** Add to pre-flight: report the marginal distribution of fan argmax over
the five arms, and gate on no arm exceeding a declared share (~40% is a
reasonable pin). Report agreement against the **majority-class baseline**
alongside uniform chance in §Measurement 2, in every case.

### F9 — Head arity is ambiguous, and the 5-way metric scores a composite no objective optimises · **Medium**

**Spec text at fault** — §Learning:
> Every fan labels all four seed arms plus no-op at a measured decision point.
> The seed head trains by directly maximizing […] `J = Σ_a π(a|s, NOW) · R_a`

§Action space defines exactly 4 seed logits, so π is a 4-way distribution and
`Σ_a` must run over 4 arms — meaning **`R_noop` is stored but plays no role in
the WHICH objective.** Yet §Measurement 2 reports "five-way agreement with the
fan argmax including restraint (chance ≈ 20%)". No head emits a five-way
distribution. Restraint comes from NOW=false, trained by a different algorithm,
on a different data distribution, against a different objective — the 5-way
metric scores a composite that nothing optimises end-to-end.

**Fix.** State the summation index explicitly in §Learning (`a` ranges over the
four seeds). Then either adopt F1's `J_now` — which puts `R_noop` back into a
full-information objective where it belongs and makes the 5-way metric
meaningful — or drop the 5-way agreement number and report only
conditional-on-acting agreement plus F4's regret.

The underlying observation is worth stating plainly: **the fan already measures
the no-op arm, so restraint carries a dense full-information label. Training
restraint by sparse online REINFORCE while its counterfactual sits labelled in
the store is the design's core waste.**

### F10 — Shared-trunk gradient interference is unmanaged · **Medium**

**Spec text at fault** — §Telemetry and policy:
> tiny causal transformer […] with the factored NOW/WHICH head above. One
> trunk.

and §Learning:
> **Interference guard:** offline WHICH updates are dense and low-variance;
> online WHEN updates are sparse and noisy.

The interference is correctly *named* and then not *managed*. High-variance
REINFORCE gradients flow into the shared trunk and the shared telemetry-
embedding MLP, corrupting the representation the dense objective depends on;
meanwhile the offline objective replays the whole store repeatedly while WHEN
sees each fresh episode once, so the trunk is dominated by WHICH by sheer
update count. No loss weighting is specified. (Relatedly: replaying the whole
accumulated store means the trunk is fit to a *t*-distribution and telemetry
distribution that shifts as the policy's germination behaviour changes —
covariate shift, mild for a full-information objective, but it compounds here.)

**Fix.** Any one of: stop-gradient from the NOW head into the trunk (train NOW
on frozen trunk features); explicit loss weights calibrated by measured
gradient norms at the trunk input, logged every update; or — cleanest — adopt
F1's fix, after which both heads train on the same dense offline objective and
the variance mismatch disappears. Under F1's restructure this finding largely
evaporates, which is a further argument for it.

### F11 — Pre-flight probes the intermediate variable, not the target · **Medium**

**Spec text at fault** — §Pre-flight validation 2:
> a linear probe […] over raw telemetry records predicts `pathology_id` well
> above chance.

`pathology_id` is an intermediate. The quantity the policy must predict is the
**fan argmax**. The §Pathology sampler table asserts a pathology→winner map
("Fan winner (by design)") that is nowhere verified — if the map is weaker than
designed, telemetry can separate pathologies perfectly while carrying little
information about which seed wins.

**Fix.** Add a second probe: telemetry → fan argmax directly, scored against
the majority-class baseline from F8. Additionally, report the realised
pathology × fan-argmax contingency table in pre-flight and compare it to the
table's design intent — that is the ground-truth version of the money chart,
and if it is not diagonal, the money chart cannot be either.

### F12 — Fossilization introduces a per-seed backward discontinuity · **Medium**

**Spec text at fault** — §Lifecycle:
> **BLENDING** […] `h + α·Δ`, α on one fixed cosine ease […]
> **FOSSILIZED:** `h + Δ`, detach dropped

During BLENDING the delta consumes `h.detach()`, so `∂h'/∂h = I` regardless of
α. At FOSSILIZED the detach drops and `∂h'/∂h = I + ∂Δ/∂h`. The **forward** is
continuous; the **backward** jumps, by an amount that differs per seed. It is
largest for `norm`, whose `Δ = GroupNorm(h) − h` has `∂Δ/∂h = ∂GN/∂h − I` —
an O(1) change — and smallest for a zero-initialised residual block.

Consequence for the reward: `R_a` confounds seed merit with a per-seed
fossilization shock. Since the shock differs systematically by arm, the fan's
ranking can be a ranking of transition shocks rather than of fit. Severity is
capped by the ≥19 fossilized epochs available to recover from a one-epoch
disturbance, so this is medium, not high.

**Fix.** Measure it rather than assume it: in pre-flight, record per-seed val-
accuracy change across the fossilization-transition epoch and require it to be
small relative to fan density. If `norm` shows a large shock, either ramp the
detach removal over one epoch or note the confound explicitly in the report —
do not let a per-arm harness artefact be read as a diagnosis result.

### F13 — Reward split and reporting split are the same set · **Low**

`R_a` is "mean val accuracy over the final 2–3 epochs," and §Measurement reads
the headline from the same quantity. Across thousands of fans on one fixed val
set, the noise is *shared* across fans, so the WHICH head can acquire seed
preferences that exploit that set's idiosyncrasies, and the headline then
reports on the set it was selected against. The effect is small — the action
space is 4 fixed seeds, so capacity to overfit is tiny — but the fix is nearly
free.

**Fix.** Split CIFAR-10's held-out data into a *reward* split (defines `R_a`)
and a disjoint *reporting* split (§Measurement, §Success criteria). Report both;
a gap between them is itself informative.

## What is correct and should not be changed

Stating these explicitly, because a review of thirteen findings should be clear
about what it is *not* attacking:

- **End-state-only reward with zero shaped terms.** This closes the failure
  class that killed the predecessor, and every finding above is compatible with
  it. Nothing here proposes a shaping term, a progress bonus, or an
  intermediate reward.
- **The baseline-invariance claim is correct.** §Learning says "A mean-fan
  baseline would not change the gradient since Σπ = 1." Verified:
  `Σ_a (R_a − b)·∇π_a = Σ_a R_a·∇π_a − b·∇(Σ_a π_a) = Σ_a R_a·∇π_a − b·∇(1)`,
  and the last term is zero. The objective is exact, not sampled, so there is
  no variance argument for a baseline either. The claim is right for the right
  reason. (Softmax parameterisation centres it automatically in any case.)
- **`R_chosen − R_noop` as the WHEN reward is zero-anchored by construction**,
  which is the correct realisation of INV-16 ("Isperia assigns no-op policy
  utility exactly zero", `docs/design/02-constitution.md:210`) at demo scale.
  F1 attacks how the quantity is *estimated and sampled*, not the anchor.
- **Retaining spike-then-crash arms rather than discarding them**
  (§Fan step 5) is correct and is the right instinct throughout.
- **The data-RNG common-future argument** (§Fan step 2) is correct and
  well-reasoned; F3 extends it, and F5 rescopes it, but the reasoning that
  "cloning RNG state alone is insufficient" is exactly right.
- **The delta contract** `h' = h + α·Δ(h.detach())` removes esper-lite's
  train/blend semantic mismatch. F12 is a transition-boundary detail inside a
  design that is otherwise cleaner than its predecessor.

## Risk Assessment

**Implementation Risk:** **Medium** pre-implementation; **High** if F1–F3 ship
unaddressed.
**Reversibility:** **Easy** now (spec-stage edits); **Moderate** after
collection begins — F3 and F5 invalidate an already-collected store, since
mismatched arms and per-fan futures cannot be repaired after the fact.

| Risk | Severity | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|
| Never-germinate collapse; `--train` produces a policy that never acts | **Critical** | F1: offline `J_now` + scheduled germination epochs during collection. Hard floor on germination rate as a tripwire, not just "outputs move". |
| Entropy term swamps or is swamped by reward; either head fails to learn and the cause is invisible | **Critical** | F2: β expressed in fan-density units, separately per head; log `‖∇_entropy‖/‖∇_reward‖` and assert a declared band. |

### N3 — `R_a = null` for diverged arms has no defined handling in either objective · **Critical**

**Spec text at fault** — §fan step 8:
> A **diverged arm** (non-finite loss) is recorded with `status="diverged"`,
> `R_a = null`, curves retained up to failure. The fan is kept

and §Learning:
> `J_which = Σ_{a∈4 seeds} π(a|s) · R_a^val`

The record format is now correct — this closes the *serialization* half that
the determinism and statistics reviewers raised, and the reasoning for keeping
diverged fans ("dropping such fans would flatter exactly the riskiest arms")
is right. But **the objective is a sum over all four arms, and `null` has no
defined value in it.** The spec never says what `J_which` does when an arm
diverged. The statistics audit asked for `R_a` to be "defined explicitly for
non-completed" arms; rev 3 defined the *status field*, not the *number the
gradient sees*.

This is the single most dangerous open item in rev 3, because the default
answer is the founding defect class. `R_a` is an accuracy in [0,1]; the
natural-looking coercion `null → 0.0` is not "bad", it is *catastrophic* —
below random guessing at 0.1 — so it would make any seed with a nonzero
divergence rate wildly repulsive on a magnitude no measurement supports. That
is precisely the reward-`None`→0 coercion recorded as one of the three
unmeasured-means-zero defects that the reset exists to make unrepresentable.
A silent `nan` propagation is worse: it destroys the update without a signal.

**Also forbidden, less obviously:** dropping the arm and renormalizing π over
survivors. It changes the objective's support per-fan, and — decisively — it
makes divergence **invisible to the policy**. A seed that wins big when it
works and diverges 15% of the time would be scored only on its wins. The
policy must be able to see the risk it is choosing.

**Fix — state the imputation in §Learning. This is an owner decision with two
defensible answers and one forbidden one:**

- *"The crashed model is what you get"* — `R_a` = val accuracy at the arm's
  last finite epoch. A real measurement, no constant, end-state-only in
  spirit. Caveat the owner should weigh: an arm that crashes at epoch 25 may
  score *above* a healthy-but-mediocre arm, so a spike-then-crash arm can be
  under-penalized.
- *"You roll back and end where you started"* — `R_a = R_noop^val` for that
  fan. Makes divergence exactly worthless rather than harmful, no constant
  introduced, and pairs naturally with the existing no-op anchor.
- **Forbidden: `0.0`, or any fixed penalty constant.** The first is the
  predecessor's coercion; the second is a shaped term and violates the scope
  pin.

Whichever is chosen, the per-seed divergence rate is already reported (§fan
step 8) — so the reader can see what the imputation is doing. The finding is
that the spec is *silent* at exactly the point where the founding defect class
recurs; the fix is "state it", not "state my preference".

### N1 — `J_now` is linear in *p*, so it trains a per-*t* threshold that is deployed as a stopping rule · **Critical**

**Spec text at fault** — §Learning:
> `J_now = p·(Σ_a π(a|s)·R_a^val) + (1−p)·R_noop^val`

against §Action space:
> At most one germination per run.

and §Evaluation:
> each policy plays its episode live (its own germination choices)

`J_now` is **linear in `p`** — both `X = Σ_a π(a|s)R_a` and `Y = R_noop` are
constants with respect to `p`. With an entropy term the closed-form optimum is
`p(s_t) = σ(A(s_t) / β_now)` where `A(s_t) = X − Y`. So the NOW head is trained
as an **independent per-epoch classifier on sign(A)**: at each *t* it answers
"does acting here beat never acting", scored against that *t*'s own no-op —
and no term anywhere compares *t* against *t'*.

Deployment is not a classifier. It is a **stopping rule**: one germination per
run, fired at the first epoch where NOW trips. Composing the two:

> **The policy germinates at the earliest epoch in the decision window where
> `A > 0`.** Not the best epoch — the first acceptable one.

Worked case: `A(s_6) = +0.01`, `A(s_12) = +0.04`. `J_now` drives `p(s_6) → 1`
and `p(s_12) → 1` (t=12 gets 4× the gradient, but both saturate). Live play
fires at t=6 and never reaches t=12. The policy banks +0.01 with +0.04
measured and sitting in the store. The lift metric records this as a success.

Three things make this worth a Critical:

1. **The mechanism is certain, and independent of the F6 question.** It
   follows from the objective's linearity plus one-germination-per-run,
   whatever the shape of `A` vs *t*. Only the *cost* is conditional — gate 6
   already measures density per scheduled epoch, so the eventual size is
   knowable, and I am not asserting a direction here either.
2. **It makes a claim the design cannot support.** Line 43–44: "inject the
   right structure **at the right time**." Nothing in `J_now` trains
   *at the right time*; it trains *at a time when acting beats not acting*.
   (Line 24's "at better moments than doing nothing" is fine — that weak
   reading is exactly what `J_now` delivers. The claim to fix is line 43–44.)
3. **The evidence to do better is already in the store, and rev 3 put it
   there.** F5's per-episode common future means fans at different *t* in one
   episode share a baseline, so `A(s_t)` and `A(s_t')` are directly
   comparable. §Episodes says so explicitly — "the store natively contains
   **now-vs-later** evidence within an episode" — and then no objective or
   metric reads it. This is banked capability going unspent.

**Fix, in ascending cost — any one is acceptable, (b) is recommended:**

- **(a) Scope the claim.** Rewrite line 43–44 to "at a moment when
  intervention beats inaction", and record "earliest-positive, not
  best-*t*" as an acknowledged limitation alongside the existing ones.
  This is honest and costs nothing. Do this regardless of whether (b) or (c)
  is taken.
- **(b) Make the schedule always draw exactly 2 ordered fan epochs per
  episode** (rather than "1–2"). Every episode then yields one paired
  now-vs-later comparison at no extra base-run cost — the marginal cost is
  4 arms, since the base run is shared. Then *report* the diagnostic:
  fraction of episodes where `A(t_late) > A(t_early)`, and the deployed
  policy's realized germination epoch versus the grid's `argmax_t A`. That
  turns an unmeasured bias into a measured one, which is the standard this
  rev has held everywhere else.
- **(c) Train the stopping rule.** With (b)'s paired data, target
  `p(s_t)` against "acting at *t* beats the best of {act later, never}"
  rather than against "acting at *t* beats never". A larger change; only
  worth it if (b)'s diagnostic shows the gap is material.

Sub-point to state in the spec either way: **§Evaluation does not say whether
live play samples `p` or thresholds it.** At `p ≈ σ(A/β_now)` and the
coefficients implied by §Learning, `p` is saturated (see N5), so the two are
nearly equivalent here — but the eval statistics differ and the choice should
not be left to the implementation.

### N4 — the val-reward / test-report split introduces a second, unmeasured ceiling on agreement · **High**

**Spec text at fault** — §Host and task:
> Val accuracy feeds telemetry and the training-time reward `R_a`. Test
> accuracy … is the **only** accuracy any reported metric uses.

and §Evaluation:
> **Oracle ceiling:** ~30 eval-grid points **refanned** (same snapshot and
> epoch, re-drawn common future) … the argmax's agreement rate across the two
> draws is the ceiling.

The partition itself is correct and closes F13. The consequence is a **units
mismatch in the ceiling**.

The training label is `argmax_a R_a^val` on 5k images; the reported score is
against `argmax_a R_a^test` on a *different, independent* 5k. Single-arm
accuracy on 5k has SE ≈ 0.0065; the paired between-arm difference is
correlated and lands nearer 0.004. If fan density (best − second) is of order
0.01, then **val-argmax and test-argmax disagree on a non-trivial fraction of
fans for pure sampling reasons** — a ceiling on measured agreement that has
nothing to do with the policy, and that the pre-registered threshold
("≥ 60% of the oracle ceiling", "≥ majority-class + 15 points") is applied on
top of without accounting for it.

The refan ceiling cannot see this. Refanning re-draws the common future but
uses the **same 5k val set every time**, so the val-sampling component is
shared across both draws and cancels. Gate 3's noise floor has the same blind
spot: it measures training stochasticity, not label noise.

**Fix — two named numbers, both nearly free because rev 3 already stores
`R_val` and `R_test` per arm (§fan step 7):**

- **Compute the oracle ceiling in test units** — the agreement of
  `argmax_a R_a^test` across the two refan draws — since reported agreement is
  scored in test units. As written the ceiling is a val-units number bounding
  a test-units score.
- **Report `P(argmax_a R_a^val = argmax_a R_a^test)`** directly from the
  stored per-arm values across all pre-flight fans. This is a direct
  measurement of how much of the *training label* is noise, and it belongs
  next to fan density in the pre-flight report.

**Related, same root:** gate 2b's telemetry → fan-argmax probe uses
val-derived features (val accuracy, val loss, loss deltas are all in the
`TelemetryRecord`) against a val-derived label, so shared val noise inflates
it. Score that probe against **test**-argmax as well, and report both.

### N2 — restraint regret and grid agreement inherit `J_now`'s per-*t* independence, penalizing correct waiting · **High**

**Spec text at fault** — §Evaluation:
> **Restraint quality:** regret vs fan-optimal *with no-op eligible* on the
> frozen grid

This metric closed F4 and is the right instrument. But it is computed at each
grid point independently: a policy that declines at *t* is scored against
`max(R_noop, max_a R_a)` **at that same *t***. So a decline at *t=6* that is
followed by a better action at *t=12* is recorded as regret of
`A(s_6)` — when it was the correct decision.

The metric therefore **systematically penalizes correct waiting**, in exactly
the direction that makes N1's earliest-positive policy look *better* than a
correctly-patient one. The two findings compound: `J_now` trains the policy to
fire early, and the restraint metric rewards it for doing so.

**Fix.** With N1(b)'s guaranteed paired fan epochs, the correct counterfactual
for a WAIT at grid point *t* is `max(R_noop, max_{t' > t} best-at-t')` within
the same episode, not `max(R_noop, max_a R_a)` at *t* alone. If N1(b) is not
adopted, then report restraint regret **only at the last grid point of the
decision window**, where "wait" and "never" coincide and the per-*t* form is
unbiased — a smaller number honestly computed beats a larger one that is
biased in the flattering direction.

### N7 — eval-time germination is not confined to the trained window · **Medium**

**Spec text at fault** — §Evaluation:
> each policy plays its episode live (its own germination choices)

§Episodes fixes collection germination to "1–2 fan epochs drawn uniformly from
the decision window", and §Pre-registered numbers gives the window as 5–15. So
`p(s_t)` is trained only for `t ∈ [5, 15]`. Nothing in §Evaluation restricts
live play to that window.

Within the window, the schedule-vs-policy distribution difference is benign
and in the favourable direction: the uniform schedule covers a *superset* of
the states the policy visits, so `p` is trained on more of the space than it
is deployed on. That is the answer to the OOD-timing question as posed — **no
adverse covariate shift inside the window.**

Outside it, there is a genuine one. If live play can fire at epoch 3 or epoch
20, `p(s_t)` there is pure extrapolation from a 2-layer transformer that never
saw a label at that *t*, and N1's earliest-positive dynamics mean it *will*
fire at the first extrapolated positive. Under a fixed 40-epoch horizon an
epoch-20 germination also leaves only 12 full-influence epochs against the
≥15 the pre-registered numbers assume.

**Fix.** One line in §Evaluation: live germination is confined to the same
decision window the exploration schedule covers, enforced by construction
(the NOW head is not queried outside it), not by the policy's discretion.

### N6 — `J_now` back-propagates into π, silently reweighting the WHICH objective · **Medium**

`J_now`'s `X` term is `Σ_a π(a|s)·R_a`, which depends on the WHICH head. So
gradient flows from `J_now` into π:

```
dJ_now/dz_i = p · π_i(R_i − R̄)        (on top of J_which's π_i(R_i − R̄))
```

The WHICH head therefore trains at an effective **`(1 + p)×`** learning rate,
while its entropy term is unchanged. Two consequences, neither stated:

- **β_which's calibration drifts during training.** It is frozen against a
  pre-flight fan density, but the reward-side term it balances grows by up to
  2× as `p` sharpens. A coefficient chosen to be correct at initialization is
  ~half strength by convergence.
- **The training distribution is reweighted.** Fans where acting is clearly
  right (`p → 1`) contribute ~2× the WHICH gradient of fans where restraint is
  right (`p → 0`). The WHICH head is trained hardest on exactly the cases
  where the NOW head has already decided, and least on the ambiguous ones.

**Fix — either is fine, state which:** stop-gradient π inside `J_now`'s `X`
term (`X = Σ_a π(a|s).detach() · R_a`), which cleanly decouples the heads'
regularization and is the recommended default; or keep the coupling and fold
`(1 + p)` into β_which's calibration explicitly.

### N5 — one fraction-of-fan-density does not regularize the two heads equally · **Medium** *(completes F2)*

**Spec text at fault** — §Learning:
> **Entropy** terms on both heads, coefficients expressed as a fixed fraction
> of the measured pre-flight **fan density**

Fan-density units are the right choice and close the F2 unit problem. On the
NOW head the result is genuinely elegant, and worth stating in the spec
because it converts coefficient-picking from guesswork into a design choice.
Both the reward and entropy gradients on the NOW logit carry the same `p(1−p)`
factor, so it cancels exactly and the equilibrium is closed-form:

```
p(s) = σ( A(s) / β_now )        # β_now is a temperature on the advantage
```

So β_now should be chosen by **target confidence**, not by a fraction: "p = 0.9
when the advantage equals one fan density" gives `β_now = fan_density / 2.2`.
Pick the number the owner actually has an opinion about.

The problem is that a *single* fraction then lands the two heads at very
different sharpness. At c = 0.05, `A/β_now = 20` puts `p` at ~1 − 2×10⁻⁹ —
fully saturated, entropy doing nothing, which is precisely the hard threshold
N1 depends on. The WHICH head at the same c also sharpens (its reward term
beats entropy by roughly an order of magnitude), but from a different starting
geometry and to a different equilibrium, because its reward gradient is damped
by `π_i` while its entropy gradient is not. The honest claim is not "WHICH
stays soft" — it is that **one fraction yields two different equilibrium
sharpnesses, neither of them chosen.**

**Fix.** Two independently named constants, `c_which` and `c_now`, each
calibrated to a stated target (for NOW, the target `p` at one fan density; for
WHICH, the target `max_a π(a)` at one fan density), both frozen and both in
`--report`.

**Also:** §Learning says entropy is reported "alongside the restraint rate so
a reader can see the regularizer is not manufacturing restraint." That guards
the wrong direction. Entropy pulls `p` toward 0.5; on restraint cases
(`A < 0`, `p → 0`) it manufactures **action**, not restraint. The diagnostic
that answers the intended question is the realized `p` distribution **split by
sign(A)** — if `p` is materially above 0 on the `A < 0` side, the regularizer
is buying action the evidence does not support.

### N8 — the frozen block does not enumerate the constants that change `R_a` · **Medium**

§Scope pins names "λ trust-region, β-ramp length, entropy coefficients,
exploration schedule" as harness properties fixed before collection. But
§Freeze discipline enumerates the frozen block as "pathology definitions, the
telemetry normalizer, entropy coefficients, and the exploration schedule", and
§Pre-flight repeats "(pathologies, normalizer, entropy coefficients,
schedule)". **λ, the stage lengths K/M/F, and the horizon are outside the
hashed block that `--eval` asserts.**

This matters for reward validity specifically because λ changes `R_a` *per
arm*: `Δ* = −g/(2λ)` shrinks each arm's delta by an amount that depends on
that arm's achievable ‖Δ‖ per unit of loss reduction, which differs sharply
between a 0.1k `norm` and a 60k `conv_heavy`. Gate 5 exists precisely to tune
λ until arms "enter within ~2×" — that is legitimate harness equalization, and
exactly why it must be inside the freeze. Stage lengths and horizon change
`R_a` through runway (the F6 mechanism).

**Fix.** Add λ, K/M/F, and the horizon to the frozen-block enumeration in
§Freeze discipline so `frozen_block_hash` covers them. One-line edit; makes
the existing `--eval` assertion actually protect the reward.

### N9 — pre-flight gate 1's binomial test uses fans as the unit · **Low** *(route: statistics)*

Gate 1 tests no-op win rate against the exact 20% null — the right null, and a
clear improvement. But the unit is the **fan**, and with "1–2 fan epochs per
episode" two fans from one episode share a base run, a pathology draw, and a
host initialization. They are not independent Bernoulli trials, so the
binomial test's CI is too narrow and the gate over-rejects.

Cheapest fix is to aggregate to one value per episode before testing. Flagged

## Part 2 — N3: conceded

**I was wrong to forbid `0.10`, and the spec's rationale is the correct
reading.** Round 2 said "Forbidden: `0.0`, or any fixed penalty constant."
That lumped two different things together, and the distinction is the whole
argument:

- **`0.0` is below the floor of the measurement scale.** No 10-class
  classifier scores 0.0; it is a fictional value, which is exactly what made
  the predecessor's `None → 0` coercion so destructive.
- **`0.10` *is* the floor.** It is what a destroyed 10-class classifier scores
  on a balanced partition. Calling it measurement rather than penalty is
  right, and it is on the same scale as every other `R_a`.

The owner's rejections of my two alternatives are also better reasoned than my
proposal of them:

- **last-finite-epoch** scores the spike-then-crash arm *at its spike*. I
  raised this as a caveat and left the choice open; rev 4 correctly weights it
  as disqualifying, because §Success criteria requires a spike-then-crash plot
  as a headline artifact. An imputation that rewards the exact anti-pattern
  the demo exists to expose is not a viable option, and I should have said so.
- **`R_noop`** hides catastrophe — the same objection I raised against
  drop-and-renormalize, applied consistently. Correct.

The finding stands as *closed by decision*, and the decision is recorded in
the spec with its alternatives, which is the durable form.

**One hardening, cheap:** `0.10` is a claim about a measurement, so measure it
rather than only assert it. A diverged arm can still be evaluated at the
horizon (argmax over non-finite logits returns a fixed index, giving the
class-0 base rate ≈ 0.10 on a balanced 5k). Have `--report` print the
**observed** end-state accuracy of diverged arms next to the convention. If
they land at 0.10, the convention is confirmed at no cost; if partial
divergence — one stage non-finite, the model still predicting — lands them at
0.03 or 0.15, the constant is visibly off and the pre-registration can be
defended or revised on evidence rather than on argument. This is a report
line, not a change to the convention or to any objective.

## Part 3 — N10: the diverged-arm convention can saturate the NOW head early · **Medium**

Raised because it is a direct interaction between two constants that are both
new in rev 4 — `0.10` (§fan step 8) and `β_now = fan_density/2.2`
(§Total training loss) — and it is invisible from either one alone.

A diverged arm sits ≈ 0.60 below a healthy arm, which at a plausible fan
density of 10⁻² is **~60× the entire discrimination signal.** Early in
offline training, before `π` has learned to avoid it, `π` is near-uniform, so
for any fan containing one diverged arm:

```
A = Σ_a π(a|s)·R_a − R_noop  ≈  (0.25·0.10 + 0.75·0.70) − 0.70  =  −0.15
p = σ(A / β_now) = σ(−33) ≈ 4.7×10⁻¹⁵
```

and the NOW head's gradient carries a `p(1−p)` factor — **4.7×10⁻¹⁵, i.e.
machine zero.** The head is not merely pushed toward "never act" on those
states; it is driven into a region with no gradient to return on. As `π`
sharpens and `π_diverged → 0`, `A` recovers to positive — but `p` must climb
back out of a fully saturated sigmoid, and it has no gradient with which to do
it.

This is **not** the round-1 starvation loop: the store is fixed and the data
supply is unaffected, so the pathology is confined to the NOW head's
optimization, not to the corpus. Severity therefore scales with the observed
divergence rate — negligible if divergence is rare, material if one seed type
diverges in ~10–15% of its arms under a targeted pathology (which is exactly
the asymmetric-failure case §fan step 8 exists to preserve).

`sg[π]` already prevents the worst version — without it, the diverged arm's
60×-scale gradient would also flow into `π` through `J_now`, amplified by `p`
— so N6's fix is doing more work here than it was asked to do.

**Fix — either, both free, neither adds a constant outside the frozen block:**

- **Order the offline training:** train `J_which` alone for a warm-up before
  enabling `J_now`. The design is fully offline and replayable, so this is a
  loop-ordering choice, not new data or new machinery. By the time `p` is
  trained, `π` already avoids the diverged arm and `A` is in its true range.
- **Anneal `β_now`** from a high starting value to its calibrated target. High
  temperature keeps `p` in a gradient-bearing region while `π` sharpens; the
  frozen target value is unchanged, so N5's calibration and N7's
  threshold-invariance both survive.

**Diagnostic, if neither is adopted:** the report already prints realized `p`
split by `sign(A)` (N5's fix). Add the divergence rate per seed type beside it
— already collected — so a reader can see whether `p`'s zero mass coincides
with fans containing a diverged arm.
